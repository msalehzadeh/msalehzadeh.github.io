## Research

My research sits at the intersection of **mathematical biology**, **epidemiology**, **ecology**, and **evolution**, using dynamical models to understand how the timing of life-history events shapes ecological and evolutionary outcomes. One thread examines the **evolution of seed masting**, a plant reproductive strategy marked by synchronized, large-scale seed production: I ask when masting is neutral or deleterious, what ecological conditions favour its evolution, and what it means for conservation and sustainable forestry under climate change. A second thread asks how disease dynamics shape life-history strategy — I model how **cyclic pathogen epidemics can favour the evolution of delayed germination**, connecting epidemiological pressure directly to evolutionary outcomes. Alongside this, I model **bark beetle infestations**, combining epidemiological and computational approaches to inform management strategies for these destructive pests. Previously, during my Master's, I applied **numerical simulations to study jellyfish swimming**, uncovering the fluid-structure interactions that drive their movement and feeding.
